Grounded before it is fluent.

Anyone can put a language model in front of a bill. Three decisions make this one safe to put in front of a patient who is frightened of the number.

01

Grounded in the actual bill

BeneBot reads the historical Explanation of Benefits before it explains any patient-specific amount. No answer about your money precedes the record it came from.

02

Math handled by software, not AI

Financial values are reconciled deterministically to a one-cent tolerance before they ever reach the conversation. If they do not balance, no numeric explanation is offered at all.

03

Two timelines kept separate

How the historical claim was processed and what benefits return today are different questions with different answers. BeneBot never uses one to answer the other.

One conversation, two people it has to serve.

For patients

Ask about your own bill, out loud, in the language you actually think in — and get an answer traceable to the claim your insurer processed.

  • Native bilingual sessions. Choose English or Spanish before you begin and hear a matching native voice throughout.
  • It asks for nothing you shouldn't give. No Social Security number, date of birth, or member ID.
  • Clearer language when you're still lost. Bounded analogies for deductible, coinsurance, allowed amount, and patient responsibility.
  • A real next step. Public help for Medicare billing problems, and a confirmed case when you want a person.

For providers

Resolve routine billing confusion without a call, and get a structured handoff when human review is genuinely needed.

  • Fewer repetitive explanation calls. The highest-volume, lowest-margin call handled on the first pass.
  • Consistent across both languages. The same grounded answer in English and Spanish.
  • Better-prepared review cases. A FHIR Task and concise Communication, not a voicemail.
  • Evidence, not vibes. Staff see which records and tools supported every answer.

Parts A & B, structural

Right now, the answer is her daughter.

“Me cobraron $2,400 por la resonancia, pero el monto permitido fue $1,100 y todavía debo $620. ¿Cómo llegaron a esa cantidad?”

“They charged me $2,400 for the MRI, but the allowed amount was $1,100 and I still owe $620. How did they get to that number?”

Today, asking that question means an adult child takes a day off work, sits on hold, and translates a conversation they don't fully understand either. The household's financial decisions get made through a guess — in a domain where guessing is expensive.

She asks. In Spanish. Out loud.

She picks her language before the session starts and hears a native voice in it the whole way through — not one voice straining across two.

She can interrupt.

“Espere — ¿qué significa monto permitido?” BeneBot stops mid-sentence, answers, and asks whether to keep going.

She isn't asked for anything.

No Social Security number, no date of birth, no member ID. The bill was already hers before the conversation opened.

She gets a real next step.

If it's a Medicare bill she may not owe, BeneBot points her at 1-800-MEDICARE, her state's free SHIP counselor, and the federal rule that makes some of these bills illegal to send.

Her provider's billing team gets something too.

The same conversation that gave her independence hands patient financial services a structured, concise case instead of a voicemail — with fewer repetitive explanation calls, and the same grounded answer in both languages.

Every number, traced to the record it came from.

Most bill explainers generate a plausible paragraph. BeneBot reconciles the actual claim adjudication in software first, refuses to speak if it doesn't balance, and cites the source and date of every figure it says out loud — in English or Spanish.

Three questions. Three sources. Never substituted.

A deductible reading taken today does not explain a claim adjudicated three weeks ago. Treating it as though it does is exactly how a patient pays something they do not owe — so BeneBot states the source and timestamp of every category, out loud, every time.

Historical

“Why do I owe this?”

ExplanationOfBenefit
Medplum · FHIR R4

The adjudication the insurer actually performed, dated to the claim. The only record that can explain a bill.

Current

“What are my benefits now?”

CoverageEligibilityResponse
Stedi · X12 270/271

A live check, timestamped at the moment it runs. Anything the payer omits stays unknown rather than filled in.

Balance

“What do I owe today?”

Invoice
Medplum · FHIR R4

The outstanding balance — neither the billed charge nor the allowed amount, a distinction behind many billing calls.

Constraints, enforced in code.

These are not prompt suggestions. They are route validation, deterministic reconciliation, and session binding.

  • Never let a current eligibility response explain or validate a historical claim.
  • Never let the language model calculate a dollar amount.
  • Never explain an exact amount before the tool call that grounds it.
  • Never claim an external action succeeded before the server confirms it.
  • Never expose a Medplum, Deepgram, or Stedi key to the browser.
  • Never open onto the chart — the session is bound to one bill.
  • Never store raw audio or persist a full transcript by default.
  • Never present a fixture as a live payer response.
